Research Philosophy & Focus

Exploring the frontiers of
reasoning and discovery.

I investigate how neural systems can escape superficial pattern matching to autonomously plan, deliberate, evaluate, and discover physical invariants.

Research Pillars
PILLAR 01 Autonomy & Planning

Agentic AI

Building systems capable of goal-directed planning, environment interaction, error reflection, and persistent state management. Moving from one-shot text completion to iterative execution environments where models navigate tool APIs, search spaces, and long-horizon tasks.

LangGraph Planning Loops Tool Use
PILLAR 02 Coordination

Multi-Agent Reasoning

Investigating distributed problem solving where distinct agents assume specialized roles (generator, critic, verifier, synthesizer). Examining communicative topologies and debate protocols that yield stronger epistemic outcomes than homogeneous scaling.

Deliberation Consensus Orchestration
PILLAR 03 Measurement

LLM Evaluation & Judges

Developing verifiable, game-theoretically sound evaluation rubrics. Addressing the pervasive failure modes of "LLM-as-a-judge" setups—including position bias, length bias, sycophancy, and self-preference—via calibration and structured verification games.

Judge Reliability Bias Mitigation Game Theory
PILLAR 04 Scientific ML

Scientific Machine Learning

Integrating physical principles and dynamical laws with modern deep learning. Utilizing neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs), autograd derivative extraction, and sparse symbolic regression (SINDy) to discover governing equations directly from trajectories.

PyTorch Autograd SINDy Dynamical Systems
PILLAR 05 Foundations

NLP & Representation Learning

Studying attention mechanisms, context compression, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and token dynamics. Understanding how internal representations capture relational knowledge and how architectural alterations affect downstream generalization.

Transformers RAG PyTorch
PILLAR 06 Geometry & Sparsity

Computer Vision & Sparse DL

Exploring representation learning in high-dimensional and non-Euclidean spaces. Designing sparse convolutional neural networks, autoencoder pretraining regimes, and pruning schedules for spatially sparse sensor grids and particle collision detectors.

Sparse CNNs Autoencoders Pruning
PILLAR 07 Inference & Ensembles

Machine Learning & Statistical Foundations

Grounding empirical models in statistical learning theory, optimization dynamics, and Bayesian inference. Applying robust tree ensembles (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) and sequential architectures to real-world geospatial, environmental, and physical datasets where calibration is paramount.

Open Research Inquiries

Current questions
I'm chasing.

These inquiries represent my ongoing intellectual investigations and reading directions, framed as formal research questions.

QUESTION 01 · MULTI-AGENT VERIFICATION

How can multiple agents collaborate to produce more reliable reasoning?

Single-model reasoning chains are notoriously susceptible to cascade errors: an early incorrect premise compounds into an erroneous conclusion. Can we formulate multi-agent debate protocols with game-theoretic verification mechanisms where counter-arguments force models to identify and self-correct subtle logical fallacies?

QUESTION 02 · NON-GAMEABLE EVALUATION

How can LLM judges be made genuinely reliable and unbiased?

Automated LLM judges frequently reward superficial verbosity and exhibit severe positional preferences. Can mechanism design, reference-free verification games, and paired counterfactual sampling establish evaluation metrics that correlate reliably with rigorous human truth?

QUESTION 03 · EQUATION DISCOVERY FROM NOISE

How can scientific structure be recovered from learned representations?

When observing complex physical systems (such as stochastic epidemic spreads or fluid dynamics), how can neural networks learn continuous state approximations and derivative surfaces so that sparse regression can reliably extract the true governing differential equations, even under measurement noise?

QUESTION 04 · DYNAMIC MEMORY & CONCEPT GRAPH EVOLUTION

How should personal knowledge representations decay, link, and evolve over time?

Human intellect is not a flat document store; it is an associative semantic network where memories evolve, connections form across distant domains, and older ideas recede until re-triggered. How can we formulate computational ontologies that mirror this organic cognitive topology?

Laboratory Ethos

Research methodology.

01

Reproducible Codebases

Every experiment, from toy simulation to large training runs, is organized with deterministic seeds, fixed environments, and complete open code repositories.

02

Honest Negative Results

Understanding what architectures fail and where methods break down is just as informative as peak metrics. Negative results clarify the actual mechanism of action.

03

Interdisciplinary Cross-Pollination

Drawing techniques from statistical physics, dynamical systems, and economics to enrich neural architectures and agentic mechanisms.
