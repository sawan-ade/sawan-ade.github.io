Scientific ML Research Deep Dive

ML4Sci: Sparse Deep Learning

Subatomic particle collision event classification using sparse convolutional neural networks, autoencoder pretraining, transfer learning, and model pruning. Developed for the Machine Learning for Science (ML4Sci) GSoC 2026 initiative.

SECTION 01

Overview

In modern experimental High Energy Physics (HEP), particle colliders (such as the Large Hadron Collider at CERN) generate petabytes of sensory sensor readings per second. Detector arrays are segmented into millions of calorimeter cells and tracking pixels. However, the vast majority of these detector cells remain quiescent during any single event, resulting in data arrays where over 95–99% of entries are exact zeros.

This research investigates end-to-end deep learning methods specifically formulated to exploit spatial sparsity. By leveraging submanifold sparse convolutions, autoencoder representation learning, and post-training parameter pruning, this pipeline attains high-fidelity event discrimination with substantial computational efficiency gains over dense baselines.

SECTION 02

The Problem

Applying conventional dense Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to high-energy physics sensor arrays encounters two fundamental inefficiencies:

CHALLENGE 01

Computational Waste on Null Manifolds

Standard 2D/3D convolutions evaluate matrix multiplications uniformly across every voxel, expending enormous GPU memory and FLOPs multiplying weights by zero in empty detector regions.

CHALLENGE 02

Submanifold Dilation ("Information Smearing")

Standard convolutions cause sparse signals to "dilate" across subsequent layers, converting an initially sparse point cloud into a pseudo-dense volume and eroding fine topological event signatures.

SECTION 03

The Approach

The system employs a three-stage methodological architecture designed to preserve and exploit sparsity throughout the representation pipeline:

1. Sparse Coordinate Representation: Input events are formatted as coordinate lists (COO tensors) storing only non-zero coordinates and associated energy deposition values, bypassing empty detector volume storage.

2. Submanifold Sparse Convolutions: Computations are strictly constrained to non-zero sites, preventing artificial spatial dilation and preserving the precise geometric localization of particle tracks and energy clusters.

3. Unsupervised Autoencoder Pretraining: A sparse convolutional autoencoder is trained to compress and reconstruct event geometries, establishing a robust feature manifold before fine-tuning for downstream binary event classification.

4. Systematic Model Pruning: Magnitude-based structured pruning is applied to identify and prune redundant kernel weights without degrading classification boundaries.
